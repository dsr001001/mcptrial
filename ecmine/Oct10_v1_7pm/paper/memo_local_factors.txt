             MEMORANDUM: THE LOCAL FACTORS BEHIND THE
                    TWIST-SYMMETRISED BOUND

                                               DHARMAJ SONI


       Abstract. This memorandum answers the question raised by both referee reports on the note
       Local component groups and the ℓ-adic valuations of the modular degree (since restructured and
       retitled Local factors of the adjoint L-function and the ℓ-adic valuations of the modular degree;
       the numbering below refers to the restructured version): are the twist-symmetrised local weights
       of its former Conjecture 1.3, now the inequality (1.3) of its Proposition 1.3, the local factors of
       the adjoint / congruence-ideal theory, and if not, what are the exact local factors? The answer is
       that the local term of a bad prime q ̸= ℓ is the ℓ-adic valuation of the Euler factor of the adjoint
       L-function at s = 1 that the naive adjoint L-function of Diamond, Flach and Guo omits, plus the
       Tamagawa exponent of the adjoint representation when the reduction is potentially multiplicative.
       The weights of the note were its shadow. The new rule is sharper and holds with no exception at
       ℓ = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 on every optimal curve of conductor at most 500000 and on 893 curves outside
       the tables (with PARI’s symmetric-square Euler factors at every bad prime, the wild primes 2
       and 3 included). The Euler term of a prime of type III can be added as well except, at ℓ = 3 only,
       when it meets a Tamagawa term at a prime ≡ −1 (mod 3): a configuration of a precise shape,
       which is exactly the one excluded by the hypotheses of Kim and Ota. Two parts of the rule are
       proved here: the Euler terms of the types I∗0 and I∗n , with no hypothesis beyond the irreducibility
       of E[ℓ] and ℓ ∤ 2N (from a formula of Diamond–Flach–Guo and an elementary twisting argument),
       and the Tamagawa terms for ℓ ≥ 5 (from Kim–Ota). The rest is reduced to a statement about
       the Σ-imprimitive adjoint Selmer group that the same theory controls.

7.3. The type III anomaly. With the III terms included, all exceptions are at ℓ = 3 and have
one shape: a prime q of type III or III∗ whose Euler factor is nontrivial with Frobenius acting
trivially on the invariant line (q ≡ 1 (mod 12) for q ≥ 5; at q = 2 the factor 1 + 2X), together
with a prime p ≡ −1 (mod 3) with t3 (E, p) ≥ 1 (a multiplicative prime, or one of type I∗n with
3 | n), and v3 (deg φE ) falls short by exactly one. On the clean configurations of the working set
(one such III prime, one such p, nothing else; 1,066 curves) the failure rate is 2%, it is 0 for q ≡ 11
(mod 12) (646 curves), and it rises with v3 (q − 1) and with v3 (p + 1) (to 12% for p ≡ 8 (mod 9));
it does not depend on whether the multiplicative reduction at p is split. For every other type the
Euler term adds to the Tamagawa terms with no exception, in particular when all the Tamagawa
primes are ≡ −1 (mod 3) (14,000 curves). Type III is the only type whose residual inertia image
mod 3 is semisimple and non-scalar; in the language of Proposition 6.1, the exceptions are the
cases where the dual Selmer correction is 1.
