                                                  1. Introduction
   Let E/Q be an elliptic curve of conductor N and let f = fE ∈ S2 (Γ0 (N )) be the newform
attached to it. Among the curves in the isogeny class of E there is a distinguished one, the
X0 (N )-optimal curve, for which the modular parametrisation φE : X0 (N ) → E has minimal
degree; throughout, E denotes this curve and deg φE its modular degree. The modular degree is
computable [9, 33], and its size is tied to deep questions (the conjectured bound deg φE ≪ε N 2+ε
is equivalent to a form of the abc conjecture, see [2, §2] and the references there), but its prime
factorisation is not understood. The purpose of this note is to describe, and for the most part
prove, a local-to-global divisibility: the ℓ-adic valuation of deg φE is bounded below by local
invariants of E at the bad primes, and the right invariants are the local factors of the adjoint
L-function.

   The classical route to the degree is the Petersson norm. Up to explicit factors deg φE is
⟨f, f ⟩ divided by the area of the period lattice of E (Zagier [35], Watkins [33]), and ⟨f, f ⟩ is
the special value L(Sym2 f, 2) up to the local factors at the bad primes (Rankin, Shimura; see
[12, Lemma 2.12]); Flach’s finiteness theorem for the symmetric square [15] is the first place
where the modular degree meets the Selmer group of Sym2 f . By a theorem of Agashe, Ribet
and Stein [2, Theorem 2.2], deg φE divides the congruence number rE , the largest integer r such
that f ≡ g (mod r) coefficientwise for some integral cusp form g of level N orthogonal to f , with
vp (deg φE ) = vp (rE ) for every prime p with p2 ∤ N . The congruence number is the congruence
ideal of f in the full Hecke algebra of level N (Lemma 3.1), and the theory of such ideals, from
Hida [18] to Diamond, Flach and Guo [12], expresses them through the adjoint Selmer group
andPthe adjoint   L-value. The L-function that appears there is the naive one: the Euler product
             2 −s
of an (f ) n , which has the true local factor at the primes of good or multiplicative reduction
and the trivial factor at the additive primes. The point of this note is that the primes at which
the naive and the true adjoint L-functions differ are exactly the primes at which the modular
degree acquires extra local divisibility, that the amount is the valuation of the omitted factor,
and that this is a consequence of the theorems of [12] once they are read for elliptic curves, with
a precise boundary at the one hypothesis they need.

   The hypothesis is the one under which Diamond, Flach and Guo prove the Tamagawa number
conjecture for the adjoint motive of f [12, Theorem 0.1]; in their notation it says that ℓ is not
in the exceptional set Sf . Theorem 1.2 is an assembly of their results. Their Theorem 3.7
identifies the length of the Σ-imprimitive adjoint Selmer group, Σ the set of bad primes, with
the valuation of the naive Σ-finite congruence ideal; their Proposition 1.4 says that this ideal is
the congruence ideal at level N times the naive Euler factors at the multiplicative primes; their
Lemma 2.1 compares the Σ-imprimitive Selmer group with the Bloch–Kato one, the local indices
being, by the formula of Fontaine and Perrin-Riou, the true Euler factors times the Tamagawa
factors; and the inequality between the congruence ideal and the congruence number is the
algebra of [10, Lemma 4.17], with the modular degree entering through [2]. The naive factors at
the multiplicative primes cancel, the true factors at the additive primes are the omitted ones, and
(1) appears. What this note adds is the reading of the local terms for elliptic curves (Lemmas 2.5
and 2.7), the observation that the assembly

1.5. History, and the statements proved without the hypothesis on Q( ℓ∗ ). The mul-
tiplicative part of (4) has a long history. Ribet and Takahashi [27, 30] compared deg φE with
the degree of a parametrisation by a Shimura curve and found the product of the nq over
the primes of the quaternion discriminant, up to primes where E[ℓ] is reducible; Pasten [22]
controls that error term. Pollack and Weston [23] conjectured, and proved for weight 2 and
square-free level when ρ̄ is ramified at two primes at least, and Kim and Ota [19] proved un-
der the hypotheses recalled in Remark 4.10, that moving a prime q into the “new” part of
the level changes the congruence ideal of fE in the full space S2 (Γ0 (N )) by exactly the Tam-
agawa exponent tq , the largest t with ρE,ℓ mod ℓt unramified at q; for multiplicative q one has
tq = vℓ (nq ) = tℓ (E, q) whether
                               Por not the reduction is split (Lemma 2.1). Theorem 1.2 contains
the inequality vℓ (deg φE ) ≥ q ∥ N tℓ (E, q) for every multiplicative prime, including the primes
q ≡ ±1 (mod ℓ) and the prime ℓ = 3, which the formula of Kim and Ota excludes; it does not
contain their equality.

6.1. Data and protocol. We used Cremona’s tables [9] in the form of the ecdata repository at
commit 25cec5e, which contain every elliptic curve over Q of conductor below 500000 with its
Kodaira symbols, Tamagawa numbers, modular degree, isogeny class, torsion and the images of
the mod ℓ Galois representations in the notation of [29]. Optimality is determined in the tables
for every class of conductor at most 400000 and for 359,009 further classes up to 500000. The
ranges are the working set (N ≤ 300000), the hold-out (300000 < N ≤ 400000), the extension
(400000 < N ≤ 500000) and the out-of-table set of Section 6.9. The component-group inequality
(4) and Conjecture 1.8 were found on the working set, written down, and then tested once on
the hold-out, which had been held back, and afterwards on the extension; nothing was adjusted
after the hold-out test. The twist-symmetrised form (5) replaced an earlier, weaker formulation
of the same phenomenon on the working set and was then tested once on the hold-out and the
extension. The Euler-factor rule came next: it was formulated on the working set from the
minima by Kodaira type (Section 6.5), the terms at 2 and 3 were fixed afterwards by PARI’s
Euler factors, and the rule was tested on all four ranges; the correction term hℓ (E) was identified
last, from the exceptions. Since the hold-out and the extension had already been used for (4)
and (5), they are a confirmation and not a blind test for Conjecture 1.3; the out-of-table curves,
generated before the rule was formulated, are. The Kodaira types and discriminant exponents
at the bad primes were recomputed with PARI/GP 2.15 [31] from the a-invariants, the Euler
factors of Sym2 f at every bad prime were computed with lfunsympow and lfuneuler, the Tate
parameters with ellinit over Qq , and fifteen modular degrees spanning the configurations were
recomputed with ellmoddegree, all agreeing with the tables. The mod 3 images of the tables
were checked against the factorisation of the 3-division polynomial (Proposition 1.7) on every
curve for which it mattered.

6.3. Calibration and sharpness. The zero counts are not tautological. Among the 366,151

the 867,578 working-set curves with a positive bound at ℓ = 3 would produce roughly 605,000
violations (summing over them the proportion of bound-0 curves whose valuation is smaller than
the bound); there are none. The bounds are attained: among working-set curves with a positive
bound, vℓ (deg φE ) equals the right-hand side of (3) in 57.5% of the cases at ℓ = 3, 75.6% at
ℓ = 5, 84.1% at ℓ = 7, 91.6% at ℓ = 11 and 92.4% at ℓ = 13, and the mean excess at ℓ = 3 is

                                Appendix A. Reproducibility
   All scripts, the pinned data commit and the result tables are in the repository https:
//github.com/dsr001001/mcptrial (directory ecmine). The symmetric-square Euler factors
are computed by scripts/step10 sym2 euler.py, the rule is checked on the whole ranges by
scripts/step10 rule.py and scripts/step11 tw boundary.py (correction term, image sta-
tistics), the Tamagawa factors by scripts/step12 tamagawa.py, the two corollary forms by
scripts/conj check.py; each runs on the full tables in minutes to hours. A single curve can
be checked against (3) in PARI/GP (version 2.15) with the following functions, which return
[vℓ (deg φE ), bound]; they are the file scripts/euler rule.gp.
rule(E, l, {iii = 0}) =
{ my(R = ellglobalred(E), P = R[4][,1], K = R[5], S = lfunsympow(E, 2), b = 0, j = E.j);
  for(i = 1, #P,
    my(q = P[i], k = K[i][2], e = 0, t = 0, vj = valuation(j, q));
    if(q != l,
      my(F = 1/lfuneuler(S, q), r = subst(F, x, 1/q^2)); \\ P_q(q^{-2}): omitted factor
      if(k >= 5, r /= (1 - 1/q^2));                     \\ multiplicative: naive one out
      if(abs(k) != 3 || iii, e = valuation(r, l)),      \\ III, III* (codes 3,-3) if iii
      if(l == 3, e = if(k == 4 || k == 2, 1,
                     if(k == -4 || k == -2, 2, if(k == -3, 1, 0)))));   \\ w_3 at q = 3
    if(vj < 0, t = valuation(-vj, l));                   \\ Tamagawa exponent
    b += e + t);
  if(iii && l == 3, b -= ns3(E));                        \\ the correction term h_3
  [valuation(ellmoddegree(E), l), b]; }
ns3(E) = my(F = factor(elldivpol(E, 3))); (#F[,1] == 2 && poldegree(F[1,1]) == 2);
   Here K[i][2] is PARI’s Kodaira code (n + 4 for In , −(n + 4) for I∗n , ±2 for II/II∗ , ±3 for
III/III∗ , ±4 for IV/IV∗ , −1 for I∗0 ), rule(E,l) leaves the type III terms out and rule(E,l,1) is
(3). For example rule(ellinit([1,1,1,-30,-76]), 3) (the curve 121a1) returns [1, 1]; for
726a1, a curve with image 3Ns and type III at 11, rule(E,3) and rule(E,3,1) both return [2,
2], the type III term and the correction cancelling; ellmoddegree needs parisizemax of a few
hundred megabytes for conductors in the thousands. The pipeline was rerun from a clean clone
and reproduced every count of Table 3. Lemma 2.4(ii) was also checked numerically, with the
script twist lemma check.gp of the repository, on 4,577 random pairs (curve, odd bad prime),
with no exception, and Lemma 2.5 against lfunsympow on every curve of the tables.

Cremona’s tables, chose the data and the protocol (a frozen snapshot of the tables, a working
set, a range held back for one-time tests, and a list of tests fixed before any was run), and decided
at each step what to pursue and what to keep. Under that direction, and in discussion with
the author, the AI system did the rest: it built the feature table and the list of known relations
to be excluded; ran the pre-registered search, which produced the candidate relation between
the modular degree and the local invariants; searched the literature and identified the results
of Ribet–Takahashi, Pollack–Weston, Kim–Ota, Agashe–Ribet–Stein and Diamond–Flach–Guo

four ranges, generated the out-of-table curves and computed their modular degrees and Tate
parameters; read the published text of [12] and assembled the proof of Theorem 1.2 from its
statements, computed the Tamagawa factor of Lemma 2.7, and proposed and wrote the proofs of
Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 and of Proposition 1.7; drafted every version of the text and the tables; and
